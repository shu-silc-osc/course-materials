# Economic Events and Accounting Fundamentals Exercises

## Transaction Recognition

Tiny Holidays encounters the following events. Determine which events should be recorded in the accounting system and explain the account effects.

A. Shelving worth $750 is ordered but has not yet been delivered.
B. After reviewing a competitor's prices, the quoted nightly accommodation rate is increased from $199 to $250.
C. A customer pays for a weekend stay upon arrival.
D. The shelving is delivered with a $750 invoice, but payment has not yet been made.

## Assumptions and Principles

Match each situation with the most relevant accounting assumption:

1. A multinational company translates transactions denominated in different currencies into Australian dollars.
2. An owner records personal expenditure as a business expense.
3. A business is not expected to remain in operation for the next twelve months.
4. A business prepares annual reports each year.

Options: economic entity, monetary unit, going concern, accounting period.

## Answers and Explanations

Transaction recognition: A and B are not recorded. C increases cash and revenue. D increases an asset and accounts payable.

Assumption matching: 1 monetary unit; 2 economic entity; 3 going concern; 4 accounting period.
