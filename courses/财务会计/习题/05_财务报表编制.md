# Financial Statement Preparation Exercises

## Opening Balances

On 1 July, a business has cash of $5,000; accounts receivable of $6,000; inventory of $23,000; prepaid expenses of $4,000; property, plant, and equipment of $25,000; land of $250,000; accounts payable of $20,000; a bank loan of $250,000; share capital of $20,000; and retained earnings of $23,000.

1. Where do these opening balances come from?
2. Which accounts have opening balances, which do not, and why?
3. Which accounting assumption is relevant?

## Classification

Classify each item as belonging to the income statement or the balance sheet: cost of sales, furniture, share capital, sales revenue, unearned revenue, retained earnings, bank loan, wages payable, wages expense, prepaid insurance, insurance expense.

## Statement Linkages

1. Which statement shows the financial success or failure of a business?
2. Why does net profit affect the balance sheet?
3. Should the balance sheet date read "for the year ended" or state a specific date?

## Answers and Explanations

Opening balances come from the prior period's ending balances. Assets, liabilities, and equity are permanent accounts and carry opening balances. Revenue, expenses, and dividends are temporary accounts and are closed at period-end. This treatment relates to the accounting-period assumption.

Income statement: cost of sales, sales revenue, wages expense, and insurance expense. Balance sheet: furniture, share capital, unearned revenue, retained earnings, bank loan, wages payable, and prepaid insurance.

The income statement reports operating performance for a period. Net profit flows into retained earnings and therefore affects equity on the balance sheet. A balance sheet is dated as at a specific date.
