# Internal Control and Fraud Exercises

## Purchasing and Payment Case

Abraham orders all factory supplies and may buy only from approved suppliers. He also receives the goods and transfers payment from the company's bank account.

1. Identify one effective control.
2. Identify the principal control weakness.
3. Design an improved control.

## RTL Cases

For each situation, identify the relevant control activity, the weakness, and an improvement:

1. The CEO reviews financial information only quarterly and is absent from the company for most of the remaining time.
2. One employee orders, receives, and pays for goods and usually does not compare deliveries with purchase orders.
3. A scheduling manager can assign himself substitute teaching work and records his hours in a paper notebook that only he can access.
4. Expired promotional codes remain usable, customers can combine two discounts, and the business does not monitor usage.
5. A warehouse manager can remove damaged inventory from the records without approval.

## Answer Guide

The approved-supplier list is an effective control. The principal weakness is the lack of segregation among ordering, receiving, and payment. These duties should be assigned to different employees, with an independent match of the purchase order, receiving record, and invoice.

For the RTL cases: increase the frequency of financial review; segregate purchasing, receiving, and payment; use electronic time records with independent verification and prevent self-scheduling; enforce expiry, single-use, and non-stacking rules for promotional codes and monitor their use; require independent approval and inventory evidence before damaged goods are written off.
