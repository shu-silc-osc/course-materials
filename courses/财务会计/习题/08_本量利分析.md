# Cost-Volume-Profit Analysis Exercises

## Yoshino Face Cream

Each box sells for $50. Ingredients cost $10, packaging $5, and the website sales fee $4 per box. Monthly rent is $1,000 and insurance is $500. Current monthly sales are 150 boxes. The business wants to earn monthly after-tax profit of $5,000, and the tax rate is 30%.

1. Calculate unit contribution margin, break-even sales volume, current profit, and the sales volume required to reach the after-tax target profit.
2. Propose three reasonable simultaneous adjustments and recalculate the break-even point and profit.

## Concept Questions

1. Which is not a fixed cost: lease payments, vehicle depreciation, hourly factory wages, or management salaries?
2. If output increases by 20% and unit variable cost remains unchanged, how does total variable cost change?
3. Where is the break-even point located on a cost-volume-profit graph?
4. Which change does not alter the break-even point: fixed cost, unit variable cost, unit selling price, or units sold?
5. How does a higher proportion of fixed costs generally affect operating risk?

## Answers and Explanations

Face cream: unit variable cost = $19; unit contribution margin = $31; break-even volume = $1,500 / $31 = 48.39, so at least 49 boxes; current profit = 150 × $31 - $1,500 = $3,150; pretax target profit = $5,000 / 0.70 = $7,142.86; target volume = ($1,500 + $7,142.86) / $31 = 278.80, so at least 279 boxes. Answers to question 2 depend on the assumptions chosen, but changes in price, variable cost, and fixed cost must be reflected consistently.

Concept questions: 1 hourly factory wages; 2 it increases by 20%; 3 at the intersection of the total revenue and total cost lines; 4 units sold; 5 operating risk increases.
