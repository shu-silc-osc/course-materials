# Financial Statement Analysis Exercises

## Ratio Calculations

1. A business reports sales revenue of $100,000 and net profit of $22,000. Calculate the net profit margin.
2. Current assets are $158,935, current liabilities are $52,234, non-current liabilities are $173,168, and total equity is $170,300. Calculate the debt-to-equity ratio as a percentage to one decimal place.
3. Cash is $17,499, accounts receivable $11,106, short-term investments $4,817, inventory $16,000, accounts payable $2,905, unearned revenue $9,134, and a ten-year loan $111,046. Calculate the quick ratio to two decimal places.

## Integrated Analysis

Lend Lease reports the following data for 2020/2021: sales revenue 11,834/9,892; cost of sales 11,361/9,132; net profit (loss) -310/222; total assets 17,748/17,000; and total liabilities 10,816/10,049. For 2019-2021, ROA was 2.74%, -1.78%, and 1.28%; ROE was 7.33%, -4.68%, and 3.21%; net profit margin was 2.83%, -2.66%, and 2.27%; receivables days were 29.04, 27.74, and 25.48; and inventory days were 54.46, 72.19, and 74.44.

1. Evaluate changes in profitability and operating efficiency.
2. What additional information is needed to assess short-term and long-term solvency?

## Answers and Explanations

1. Net profit margin = 22%.
2. Debt-to-equity ratio = ($52,234 + $173,168) / $170,300 × 100% = 132.4%.
3. Quick ratio = ($17,499 + $11,106 + $4,817) / ($2,905 + $9,134) = 2.78. The long-term loan is excluded from current liabilities.

Integrated analysis: profitability recovered from a loss in 2021 but remained below the 2019 level. Lower receivables days indicate improved collection, while higher inventory days indicate slower inventory turnover. Useful additional measures include the current ratio, quick ratio, debt-to-equity ratio, and interest coverage ratio.
