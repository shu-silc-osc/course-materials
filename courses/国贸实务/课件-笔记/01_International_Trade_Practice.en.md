# International Trade Practice | Study Notes

> [!abstract] Review objective
> Connect contracts, trade terms, transport, insurance, payment, documents, inspection, and dispute resolution. For each transaction, identify obligations, costs, risk transfer, and required documents.

## 1. Transactions and contracts

- Negotiation commonly covers inquiry, offer, acceptance, and contract formation. Review when an offer becomes effective, how withdrawal or revocation works, when acceptance takes effect, and how amendments affect the contract.
- The sales contract should specify product description, quality, quantity, packing, and shipping marks. Quality may be defined by sample, specification, or grade. FAQ commonly means Fair Average Quality; GMQ means Good Merchantable Quality.
- A “more or less 5%” clause may suit bulk goods; specify who chooses the tolerance and how the price is calculated.
- Neutral packing omits identifying marks of the producer or country. Shipping marks can identify cargo through numbers, destination, and package sequence.

## 2. Incoterms, transport, and risk

- **FOB and CIF:** identify the shipment port, risk-transfer point, freight and insurance arrangements, and export/import clearance duties. Under CIF, the seller arranges the stipulated insurance; risk does not wait until arrival at the destination port to transfer.
- **DAP, DDP, and DAT/DPU:** distinguish delivery at destination, unloading, and import clearance. DAT is a term from an earlier Incoterms edition; check which edition the course uses.
- A negotiable ocean bill of lading may be transferred through its consignee wording and endorsement and may serve as a document of title. An air waybill is generally not a document of title. Multimodal documents allocate responsibility according to the transport arrangement and document issued.
- Watch for master versus house bills, transshipment, through transport, and differences between issuance and shipment dates. Endorsement depends on the wording and nature of the document.

## 3. Cargo insurance

- Check insured risks, exclusions, duration, insured amount, and claim documents.
- The materials list FPA, WPA, and All Risks. Coverage generally broadens across these categories, but the actual policy wording controls.
- Warehouse-to-warehouse wording describes the beginning and end of the insured transit period; it does not make every transport risk automatically covered.
- Constructive total loss, general average, sue-and-labour expenses, and claim conditions are common calculation and classification topics. In a CIF transaction, the buyer with the insured interest commonly makes the claim, subject to the named insured, policy rights, and contract terms.

## 4. Bills of exchange, letters of credit, and settlement documents

- A bill of exchange is drawn by a drawer and directs a drawee to pay a payee. Distinguish sight and time drafts, commercial and bank drafts, drawer, drawee, and payee.
- A letter of credit is an independent bank undertaking to pay against documents. Examination focuses on whether the presented documents comply with its terms. Amendments must follow the applicable credit rules and involve the relevant parties; one party cannot change the credit unilaterally.
- Confirmed, transferable, back-to-back credits, and negotiation are common comparison topics. Transfer conditions and the number of transfers depend on the governing rules and credit terms; do not infer them from shorthand notes alone.
- Match the commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, insurance policy, bill of lading, and draft to the transaction. Endorsement requirements depend on the document type and its wording.

## 5. Packing, inspection, origin, and trade measures

- Wooden packaging may require fumigation or phytosanitary evidence. The issuing body for an inspection certificate depends on the country, product, and contract.
- A certificate of origin establishes origin. Chambers of commerce, CCPIT, or statutory authorities issue different certificate types according to their authority. Form A is associated with the Generalized System of Preferences; follow the course’s specified examination convention.
- Tariffs include ad valorem, specific, and compound duties. Non-tariff measures include quotas, licences, technical or intellectual-property requirements, local-content requirements, and government-procurement rules.
- Anti-dumping duties follow a dumping investigation and injury determination. Countervailing duties address countervailable subsidies. A low import price or an export tax rebate alone does not establish that a duty must be imposed.

## 6. Dispute resolution and review

An arbitration clause should address applicable rules, seat or institution, and language. The binding effect of an award depends on the governing law and rules. Force majeure, amendments, exchange-rate risk, and hedging must be analysed against the contract and facts.
