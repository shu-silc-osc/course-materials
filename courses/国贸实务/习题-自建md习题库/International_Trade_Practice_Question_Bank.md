# International Trade Practice Question Bank

## How to Use

Questions and answers are written in English. Cover the answer while solving. For document and Incoterms questions, state the relevant condition before choosing an answer.

## Trade Terms and Risk

### Multiple-Choice Questions

1. Under a CIF contract, which statement is generally correct?
   A. The seller must insure the goods, and risk transfers only when they arrive at destination.  
   B. The seller arranges the required insurance, while risk transfers at the shipment stage specified by the applicable Incoterms rule.  
   C. The buyer must arrange export clearance.  
   D. The seller bears every risk until the buyer resells the goods.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

B.  CIF requires the seller to arrange insurance, but the risk-transfer point is at shipment, not arrival.

</details>

2. Which Incoterms responsibility is most closely associated with DDP?
   A. The buyer handles import clearance and duties.  
   B. The seller delivers without import clearance.  
   C. The seller assumes import-clearance responsibility and applicable duties, subject to the rule and contract.  
   D. The buyer unloads at the seller’s premises.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

C.  DDP places the broadest import-clearance obligation on the seller among these options.

</details>

3. Which document is generally not a document of title?
   A. Negotiable ocean bill of lading  
   B. Air waybill  
   C. Order bill of lading  
   D. Endorsed negotiable bill of lading

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

B.  An air waybill is generally a non-negotiable transport document, not a document of title.

</details>

4. A specific tariff is assessed as:
   A. A fixed amount per unit imported  
   B. A percentage of customs value  
   C. A share of the exporter’s profit  
   D. A fixed amount per transaction regardless of quantity

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A.  A specific duty is charged per physical unit; an ad valorem duty is based on value.

### Short-Answer Questions

</details>

5. Explain the difference between a specific tariff and an ad valorem tariff.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A specific tariff is a fixed charge per unit, such as dollars per barrel. An ad valorem tariff is a percentage of the customs value.

</details>

6. What information should be checked before deciding whether a bill of lading requires endorsement?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Check the consignee wording, whether the document is negotiable, the applicable transfer method, and the contract or bank’s documentary requirements.

</details>

7. What does warehouse-to-warehouse insurance wording describe, and what does it not guarantee?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It defines the insured transit period’s start and end. It does not remove policy exclusions or automatically cover every event.

</details>

8. Distinguish an anti-dumping duty from a countervailing duty.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

An anti-dumping duty follows a finding of dumping and injury under the applicable investigation. A countervailing duty addresses countervailable subsidisation and injury. Neither follows automatically from a low import price or a tax rebate.

</details>

9. Give three examples of documents that may be required in an international sale and state their purpose.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A commercial invoice states the transaction and value; a packing list describes package contents; a certificate of origin identifies origin. Depending on the transaction, a bill of lading, insurance policy, inspection certificate, and draft may also be required.

</details>

10. What is the difference between an ocean bill of lading and an air waybill with respect to title and transfer?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A negotiable ocean bill may function as a document of title and be transferred by endorsement and delivery. An air waybill is generally non-negotiable and does not function as a document of title.

11. A buyer receives a CIF shipment damaged during the ocean voyage. Identify the first documents and facts to check before making an insurance claim.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Check the insurance policy/certificate, named insured or loss-payee rights, the CIF contract, bill of lading, survey or damage evidence, notice deadlines, and the policy’s covered risks and exclusions. The seller’s procurement duty does not by itself determine who may claim; the policy rights and contract control.

</details>

12. A letter-of-credit presentation contains an invoice and transport document that do not match the credit’s description. What should the beneficiary do next?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Compare each discrepancy with the credit and governing documentary rules, contact the relevant bank and applicant promptly, and seek a valid amendment or waiver where available. Do not alter the credit unilaterally or assume the bank will ignore a discrepancy.

</details>

13. A contract states “more or less 5%” for a bulk commodity but does not identify who chooses the quantity shipped. What is the drafting risk?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The allocation of the tolerance decision may be disputed. The contract should identify who may choose the quantity, the calculation base, and the pricing/payment treatment.

</details>

14. A wooden crate is used for an export shipment. What documentary issue should the shipper investigate?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Determine whether the destination and commodity rules require treatment, marking, or a fumigation/phytosanitary certificate for the wooden packaging.

</details>

15. A firm wants to reduce exposure to an expected foreign-currency payment. Name a general financial approach and one condition for using it.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The firm may use a forward contract or another permitted hedge. It must identify the currency, amount, timing, and exposure, and ensure the hedge matches the underlying payment and applicable rules.

</details>
