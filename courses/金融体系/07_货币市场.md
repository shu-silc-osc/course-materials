#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Suppose that the 90-day yield BBSW just rose from 3.8% p.a. to 4.10% p.a. What does this
imply for (i) prospective investors and (ii) investors holding money market securities?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A rate rise is good news for prospective investors since it increases the interest return from
future investments. However it is bad news for existing investors because it lowers their
selling price (given the inverse relationship between prices and interest rates) if they choose to
sell before maturity.

</details>

2. Suppose you expect that short-term yields are about to fall. Suggest an investment
strategy that would profit from this expectation.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The expectation of falling yields is equivalent to an expected price rise. Therefore a simple
strategy would be to purchase money market securities now and then sell them after the
market yields fall. If your expectations are correct the change in yield will produce a capital
gain that allows you to achieve a return above current market yields. As price risk is
positively related to the term to maturity, this capital gain will be larger the longer the
security’s term.

</details>

3. Explain the risks and possible returns from an investment in the money market given
the 90-day BBSW is currently 4.75 per cent.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

If a security is purchased at a yield of 4.75 per cent, this is the yield that will be earned if the
security is held until maturity. However, if the investor sells before maturity they are exposed
to price risk. This is the possibility that yields have risen, causing prices to fall. Selling at a
lower price reduces the holding-period yield to less than 4.75 per cent because of a capital
loss. Of course there is also the potential for capital gains arising from falls in market yields
which would push up returns above 4.75 per cent.
As the money market is a debt market, there is also some chance of default (referred to as
credit risk). However, in Australia the likelihood of default is very low because of the low
credit risks of issuers and acceptors. Nevertheless, because of different credit risks, securities
will trade at different yields. So, for example, an investor will earn a higher return on lower
rated commercial paper than on NCDs or BABs.

</details>

1. What is the BBSW and how is it ‘discovered’?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

it serves the price discovery role for short-term funds in Australia. It reflects the current level

at 10 a.m. each day for a range of maturities, with the resulting rates available soon after.

</details>

### Contribution to the flow of funds

#### True/False Questions

1. Bank-accepted bills and negotiable certificate of deposits are used to raise funds for the banks.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
Banks are the principal borrowers from the market through their issue of negotiable
certificates of deposit (NCDs). Bank-accepted bills (BABs) are bills of exchange that raise
funds for their issuer once they have been ‘accepted’ by a bank. Both NCDs and BABs are

raise funds for banks while BABs raise funds for other borrowers.

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. How does the RBA implement monetary policy?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The RBA first announces its monetary policy decision – that is, whether or not the cash rate is to
change. This occurs at 2.30 p.m. after a meeting of the RBA’s board on the first Tuesday of each
month (except January). The following day the RBA enforces the new cash rate (if changed) through
its open market operations. Decisions to increase the cash rate are accompanied by the sale of
securities by the RBA (to reduce liquidity in the overnight market) and decreases in the cash rate are
implemented by security purchases by the RBA. These transactions occur through ESAs and then flow
onto the overnight interbank market as ADIs restore their desired ES balances. The change in
overnight interbank balances causes the price of funds in that market (the cash rate) to change.

</details>

4. What are the main differences between bills of exchange and promissory notes?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Bills of exchange are accepted (by a bank, for a fee) so as to enhance the bill’s credit standing
(reduce its risk) because both the issuer and acceptor are promising to redeem the security.
Some of the major banks are the main acceptors and as ‘prime’ banks, their BABs trade at a
common yield.
Promissory notes are not guaranteed or accepted by a third party. Issuers are very
creditworthy and include the Treasury (T-notes), highest-grade companies (CP) and banks
(NCDs). Yields for promissory notes will vary between issuers to reflect differences in credit
risk and in their liquidity.

</details>

2. Explain the role of dealer panels in the commercial paper market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Dealer-panels undertake the primary market issue of commercial paper on a best-efforts basis.
This involves them providing advice to issuers about the amounts and terms being issued as
well as selling the securities to investors. They also make the secondary market for the paper.
That is, members of the panel agree to quote bid and offer yields for the paper, and must trade
when a quote is accepted.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Explain the motive for a tight monetary policy and how it is implemented.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Monetary policy is tight (i.e. rates are rising) when the RBA is attempting to contain the inflation rate
to its target rate of 2 to 3 per cent per annum.
It is implemented through the announcement of the change in the cash rate and then through open
market operations to enforce the change. Increases in the cash rate are enforced by the sale of
government securities, which reduces the aggregate amount of exchange settlement funds. These
accounts are restored to their desired balance by withdrawals from the overnight interbank market
causing the rate in that market (the cash rate) to rise.

</details>

3. Money market dealer’s quotes reflect their desire to ‘buy low’ and ‘sell high’ so their bid

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
Dealers aim to profit from their trading activities by selling securities for a higher price than for what
they were purchased – i.e. buy for a low price/sell for a high price. However their quotes are in the
form of yields and yields are inversely related to price. Therefore their bid yields will exceed their
offer yields.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
Dealers earn income from the trading services they provide as well as interest on the securities
they hold. Their trading income is earned from trades that are made at their bid and offer rates.
Keep in mind that in the money market a dealer’s bid yield will be higher than the dealer’s
offer yield. This will result in the dealer buying low and selling high (since yield and price are
inversely related).

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Explain the activities of dealers in the money market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Money market dealers trade on their own behalf with other dealers and wholesale clients. They trade
from their own trading rooms by phone or electronically and in accordance to protocols specified by
AFMA. They quote bid and offer yields to 2 decimal points when contacted by another dealer. Dealers

hold an inventory of securities in order to provide counterparties with immediacy. They aim to earn a
profit on their trading activities and also earn interest on the securities they hold.

</details>

2. Explain the settlement arrangements in the money market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Trades are settled on a T + 0 (same day) basis – that is, morning trades are settled in the
afternoon and afternoon trades are settled the next morning. Settlement is arranged by the
dealer’s back offices as well as the clearinghouse Austraclear. The seller of securities lodges
the transaction details and these are confirmed by the buyer. Payment is on a RTGS basis and
then ownership of the securities is transferred.

</details>
