### BAB futures as a hedge instrument

#### True/False Questions

1. BAB futures contracts can be used to manage the credit risk posed by the borrower.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
The credit risk posed by the borrower is the risk that the face value of the BAB is not paid,
whereas futures contracts manage the market risk posed by unexpected movements in the
BBSW.

</details>

2. BAB futures can hedge an interest rate risk exposure by providing borrowers and lenders
with access to forward interest rates.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

T
The price of a futures contract depends on the forward yield for its settlement date. Taking a
futures position establishes a value based on the forward yield, and its subsequent closeout
generates a cash settlement that offsets the variation between the prevailing 90-day interest
rate and the forward rate.

</details>

term interest rates is the three-year Commonwealth Treasury bonds futures contract.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

4. Borrowers would buy BAB bills to set their forward borrowing rate.

> Difficulty: Advanced

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
Borrowers would sell BAB futures to establish a forward borrowing rate, and lenders would
buy BAB futures to establish their forward lending rate. This means that borrowers would set
their hedge at the market’s bid price, and lenders would set their hedge at the market’s offer
price.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Describe the trading in BAB futures required to hedge a one-year $50 million bill facility
using 90 day bills given it is now February and the facility will begin in March. What will be
the effective cost of funds established for the facility if in February the price of March, June,
September and December BAB futures are 96.00, 95.80, 95.50 and 95.00 respectively.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In order to hedge the bill facility the borrower will need to establish a strip hedge in short
BAB futures contracts. Specifically, in February they will:
sell 50 March BAB futures at 96.00,
sell 50 June BAB futures at 95.80,
sell 50 September BAB futures at 95.50 and
sell 50 December BAB futures at 95.00.

These contracts will then be closed out just before their settlement date and at the same time
as the BABs are issued or rolled over in the facility. Specifically, they will:
buy 50 March BAB futures in March;
buy 50 June BAB futures in June;
buy 50 September BAB futures in September; and
buy 50 December BAB futures in December.
The closeout trades will produce a profit or loss on the futures position which offsets
unexpected variations in the cost of funds under the bill facility. In February the borrower has
established a cost of funds equal to 4%, then 4.2%, then 4.5% and then 5% for the four 90-day
bill issues.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Explain why basis risk is likely to be a problem when hedging long-term interest rates
using futures contracts.

> Difficulty: Advanced

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Basis risk is the chance that a hedge instrument will not precisely manage a risk exposure. It is
likely to occur when hedging long-term rates with futures contracts because the actual interest
rate exposure does not match the futures contract features. Some of the standardised features
of the Treasury bonds futures contracts are a term of three or 10 years to maturity, a $100 000
face value, a coupon rate of 6 per cent and four contract dates per annum. The closer the
underlying exposure is to these features, the more accurate the hedge will be.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Why do most futures contracts require mandatory cash settlement? What does this entail?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Mandatory cash settlement means the futures contract specifies that the contract must be cash
settled, rather than settled through the delivery of the contract item. Most contracts require
cash settlement because:
sometimes the contract item does not exist (such as a share price index);
cash settlement avoids problems associated with guaranteeing the quality of the
item (such as with agricultural items); and
cash settlement is more convenient and less costly than physical delivery.
On the contract’s settlement date, any remaining open futures positions are cash settled,
meaning the cash difference between the contract’s value on its settlement date and its value
when it was traded is paid by the losing party to the winning party.

</details>

### Futures market participants

#### True/False Questions

1. Speculators buy futures contracts whereas hedgers sell them.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
Speculators hold futures positions for the purpose of making a profit. They will hold short
positions when they assess that the values of futures contracts are too high (i.e. they expect
their values will fall) and hold long positions when they expect values will rise. Hedgers use
futures contracts to counter-balance their exposure to the contract item in another market.

</details>

2. An arbitrage trade should generate a risk-free profit.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

T
An arbitrage profit arises when an item trades at different prices and the difference exceeds
the transaction costs of buying at the low price and selling at the high price. The buy and sell
trades are made simultaneously and thus the profit is risk-free.

</details>

3. Futures are low-risk, low-return investments because no funds are invested.

> Difficulty: Advanced

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

F
The risk and return depend on the movement in the value of the futures contract. The absence
of an investment of funds implies that futures are leveraged investments and thus are high-risk
and high-return investments.

</details>

4. Futures investment can offer higher returns than equivalent investment in a physical
market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

T
One advantage of the futures transaction is that the investor does not have to make the cash
outlay that is required in the physical market. Hence, the rate of return from a successful
futures investment can be many times that of an equivalent investment in the physical market.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. When will a futures trader be required to pay additional funds into their margin account?

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Margin accounts have a maintenance level, which is usually 75 per cent of the amount of the initial
margin payment. The clearinghouse marks-to-market each trader’s account daily, unrealised increases
or decreases in value are added or deducted to the trader’s margin account. Should a trader’s losses
cause their margin balance to fall below the maintenance level, they will be required to pay a
maintenance margin before the commencement of business on the next trading day.

</details>

2. Why might a speculator prefer to trade futures contracts rather than the underlying asset?

> Difficulty: Advanced

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A significant advantage of futures contracts is they allow a trader to establish a position in the
market without the cash outlay that is required in the physical market, and can therefore
produce much higher (and lower) rates of return. Futures contracts also allow the trader to
profit from a predicted fall in the price of an asset by establishing a short position.
Furthermore futures can give an investor an exposure to a variable that is otherwise hard to
access, such as the cash rate (using 30-day interbank cash rate futures contracts) or the
ASX200 (via SPI futures). Speculators value liquidity and are therefore much more likely to
trade the futures contracts for which turnover is high.

</details>

1. Explain the similarities and differences between forward contracts and futures contracts.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In common with forward contracts, futures contracts specify:
the item being traded;
the contract’s settlement date;
how the contract can be settled; and
the settlement price agreed between the buyer and seller.
The contracts differ in that futures contracts:
are only over contract items specified by the exchange;
are only with settlement dates specified by the exchange;
are mostly cash settled (based on the change in the price of contract item) rather than settled

can be traded prior to their settlement date allowing the trader to avoid the contract’s

2. How do traders establish a ‘long’ or ‘short’ futures position? Explain how these positions can
be subsequently ‘closed-out’.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A long position results from the trader buying the futures contract. This trader owns the
contract and has an obligation to buy the contract item at the settlement date at the agreed
price (where the contract is deliverable). The long position can closeout any time prior to the
contract’s settlement date. This is achieved by the long position taking an offsetting short
position (i.e. selling the number of contracts they hold). Closeout will produce a profit or loss
depending on the difference between the price the contract was bought and sold at.
A short position results from the trader selling the futures contract. This trader has contracted
to sell the contract item to the contract’s buyer on the settlement date at the agreed price
(where the contract is deliverable). The short position can closeout any time prior to the
contract’s settlement date. This is achieved by the short position taking an offsetting long
position (i.e. buying the number of contracts they previously sold). Closeout will produce a
profit or loss depending on the difference between the price the contract was sold and then
bought at.
Where contracts are not deliverable, positions not closed-out earlier will be closed-out (i.e.
cash settled) by the clearinghouse on the contract’s settlement date.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The futures market performs the risk-transfer function. This enables traders to manage (i.e.

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Explain the role played by the clearinghouse in a futures market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The clearinghouse in the futures market is ASX Clear (Futures). It arranges the settlement of
transactions, including mandatory closeout when trading in a contract ceases (at the settlement
date), and it guarantees the performance of futures contracts. The clearinghouse manages
traders’ default risk through novation and its system of margin payments.
Novation is an arrangement whereby the clearinghouse acts as a counterparty to all traders
and thus can guarantee that default by one party to a futures contract does not affect the other
party.
The clearinghouse protects itself against default by ‘losing’ traders through a system of
deposits called margin payments. An ‘initial’ margin is payable when a position is opened.
This may be followed by further ‘maintenance’ margins to provide security against unrealised
losses; these are determined by the clearinghouse in a daily process known as ‘marking-to-
market’.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
