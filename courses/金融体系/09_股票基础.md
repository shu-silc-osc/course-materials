#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Discuss the process of an accelerated rights issues.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Accelerated rights issues were introduced during the Global Financial Crisis in order to speed
up the process of fundraising via rights issues. There are two parts to an accelerated rights
process: first, an accelerated issue to institutions, followed by a non-accelerated offer to retail
investors. The advantage of the two-stage process is that the issuing company receives the
majority of its funding from institutional investors quickly, thus reducing the risk of a funding
shortfall. An accelerated offer generally involves bookbuilding offers to institutions over a
short one- or two-day period and is similar to a traditional share placement.
Once the accelerated first round of fund raising is complete, the issuing company offers the
rights to retail investors over a longer period. Retail investors use this longer period to decide
whether or not to take up their entitlements. The two-stage process allows a balance between
allowing all shareholders the opportunity to participate in an issue and the need for companies
to rapidly secure additional funding. A positive side effect of the accelerated rights process is
that it reduces underwriting costs as the risk of a funding shortfall is reduced by the bookbuild
that places the majority of shares at a price that satisfies the market.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Compare the general characteristics of ordinary and preference shares and describe the
other conditions or features that can be attached to preference shares.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Ordinary shares represent an ownership interest in a company. They would typically be
perpetual, transferable, have limited liability and entitle their owners to distributions of profit
that are made as dividend payments and to vote at annual general meetings. As there is no
upper limit to possible profits and dividends payments, ordinary shares have potential for
capital gains.
Preference shares also have many of these features except (i) the shares pay a fixed dividend
payment that has priority over dividend payments to ordinary shareholders and (ii) preference
shareholders do not have (or have restricted) voting rights. Because dividend payments to
preference shareholders are fixed they are much less likely to achieve capital gains.
In addition, preference shares can be issued with other features. The most common are:
‘non-participating’ – where shareholders would not receive bonuses such as special
dividends or issues of bonus shares;
‘cumulative’ – the company promises to subsequently make up any missed dividend
payments;
‘converting’ – the preference shares will convert to ordinary shares on a specified date at a
specified ratio; and
‘redeemable’ – the company has the right to buy them from their holders.

</details>

1. Describe the processes involved in both a ‘top-down’ and also a ‘bottom-up’ approach to
security analysis.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The top-down approach starts with an assessment of the economy’s future prospects followed
by an assessment of the sector and industry in which the firm operates. Analysts would
consider the competition a firm faces and then estimate the effects on the firm’s future
earnings. Finally, analysts would assess the ability of each firm’s management to implement
their firm’s plans and cope with unexpected events.
The bottom-up approach focuses on the analysis of individual shares rather than a review of
the industry or economy. This involves a thorough study of the company in question,
including an assessment of a firm’s financial position from its financial statements.

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Describe the purpose and process of a rights issue.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A rights issue is a method of raising a substantial amount of new equity capital. The rights are
issued to existing shareholders in proportion to the number of shares owned, and so the rights
issue need not dilute their degree of ownership. Rights provide their holder (initially this
would be a shareholder) with the right to purchase shares at the set subscription price on the
subscription date. Generally, rights can be sold prior to the subscription date. The main steps
in the process are:
The announcement of the offer, after which the shares trade cum-rights.
The ex-rights date, when the rights are assigned to shareholders. The shares then
trade ex-rights and the rights can be traded separately.
The subscription date, when the rights are exercised or allowed to lapse.

</details>

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The disadvantages are first, that the issue of new shares to the public dilutes the ownership
(and control) of the original owners. Secondly, conducting an IPO is expensive and the fees
reduce the net proceeds from share sales. Next, it becomes more likely the company will incur
agency costs resulting from the separation of ownership and control as it becomes bigger.

market and this can give decision making a short-term bias.
The advantages of the IPO are the ability to raise large amounts of new equity capital. As
equity is a permanent source of funds, this enhances the firm’s financial strength. The listing
of shares on the exchange greatly enhances their liquidity. Listed firms are also better able to
remunerate management and employees because of their ability to provide share-related
bonuses.
