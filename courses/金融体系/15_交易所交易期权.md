#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Discuss the features of ASX share options.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

ASX share options can be either American-form or European-form options. Their other
features include:
Contract item: options are traded on a selection of the most actively traded shares.
Contract size: one option contract covers 100 of the underlying shares.
Expiry date: contracts expire on the Thursday before the last business Friday in the
expiry month. The expiry months are the next serial months, with standard quarterly
(Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) expiry months thereafter.
Exercise price: these are set by the clearinghouse with the aim of having a range of
exercise prices distributed around the current share-price.
Option premium: this is the option’s price and is established through market trading.

</details>

2. Is the ASX options market a broker or a dealer market?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The market is an order-driven market, where brokers trade on behalf of their clients and earn a
commission. However, to add to the market’s liquidity, the ASX authorises some dealers to
act as market makers for particular option series.

</details>

3. Compare the system of margin payments used in the futures market with that used in the
options market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

In the futures market, holders of both the short and long positions must make an initial deposit
into their margin accounts, with further payments made by the holder of the losing position
that are based on the movements in the value of the contract. In the option markets, only the
holder of the short position is required to make margin payments (or post some other form of
collateral), since the long position will let the option lapse rather than exercise at a loss.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Describe the rights and obligations of call and put option buyers and sellers.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A call option provides the buyer with the right (but not the obligation) to buy the underlying
asset from the option seller by paying the exercise price on (or before) the expiry date. The
seller of the call option has the obligation to sell the underlying asset to the option buyer if the
buyer chooses to exercise their option.
A put option provides the buyer with the right (but not the obligation) to sell the underlying
asset to the option seller for the exercise price on (or before) the expiry date. The seller of the
put option has the obligation to buy the underlying asset from the option buyer if the buyer
chooses to exercise their option.

</details>

2. Distinguish between the exercise price, the strike price and the option premium.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The exercise price and the strike price are the same thing. This is the price at which an option
can be exercised by the option holder on (or before) the expiry date. For a call option, the
holder has the right to buy the asset for the exercise/strike price, whereas a put gives the
holder the right to sell for the exercise/strike price.
The option premium is the price paid by the option buyer to the seller to acquire the option
contract.

</details>

3. Who has the ‘long’ and the ‘short’ position in an option contract?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The option buyer (or holder) has acquired the option and they hold the long position. It is this
position that has the right, but not the obligation to exercise the contract.
The option seller (or writer) has sold the option and they have the short position. They are
obligated to settle the contract if it is exercised by the holder.

</details>

4. Distinguish between American and European options.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Despite the names, the differences in these options are not where they are traded. An
American option is an option that may be exercised by the holder of the option at any time up
to, and including, the exercise date. In contrast, European options can only be exercised on the
expiry date.

</details>

5. Compare the income potential of call option sellers and buyers.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

An option seller’s maximum income is the premium which they are able to keep in total if the
option lapses at expiry. However to earn the premium the option seller takes on the risk of
large losses.
The buyer has unlimited upside potential because the option is able to fully capture the benefit
of higher prices above the share price and there is no limit to how high a share price could go.
The worst outcome for the buyer is the options lapse at expiry in which case they have lost the
premium paid.

</details>

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Compare the hedging capabilities of futures and options.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It is theoretically possible to achieve ‘perfect’ hedging with futures, meaning that you pay for the
futures position to protect your downside by foregoing any prospect of benefiting from the potential
upside.
The option’s exercise price is a forward price, so that an option can be used for hedging. However
paying the option’s premium means that it will not provide a perfect hedge (since the premium adds to
the exercise price when hedging with calls, and reduces the exercise price when hedging with puts).
Moreover, the option retains some possibility of capturing upside from future movements in the share
price.

</details>

intrinsic value from share price movements above the exercise price. Put option values are
negatively related to the share price because they have intrinsic value when share prices
are below the exercise price.
Both call and put option values are positively related to the option’s time to expiry because
greater price movements (that increase intrinsic value) are likely over longer periods.
Both call and put option values are positively related to the expected volatility in the share

possibility of substantial intrinsic value.

call is maximum of (S – X, 0). Thus the $12 option has intrinsic value of $0.55, the $12.50
option has intrinsic value $0.05, but the $13 option has none. Even though the options have
the same expiry date, they will have different amounts of time value. ‘At-the-money’ options
have the greatest time value. The $12.50 option is the closest to being at the money, so its
time value will exceed those of the other options.
