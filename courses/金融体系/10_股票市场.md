### Competition for the provision of equity trading

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Identify and briefly explain the competitive pressures faced by the ASX.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Three developments that threaten the ASX’s position in Australia are the entry of Chi-X, the
emergence of ‘dark pools’ as alternative trading venues and the possibility of the entry of a
competitor to CHESS.
Chi-X is owned by a Japanese bank. In 2011 it was granted a licence to operate a new
exchange in Australia to operate as a competitor to the ASX. Chi-X specialises in
providing services to wholesale clients, including a trading platform that supports
algorithmic trading. Currently it facilitates trading in the shares of about 300 of the largest
listed companies.
‘Dark pools’ refers to share trades that are arranged privately by brokers rather than through
an exchange. This facilitates very large trades with minimal impact to the market price and
avoids the exchange’s trading fees. Brokers may also ‘cross’ the trade of their retail clients
without their knowledge. The trade is reported to the market after its completion. The
amount of dark pool trading has increased recently because of technological advances.
At present, the ASX’s CHESS is the sole share clearing and settlement system however the
government have indicated this situation will be reviewed.

</details>

1. Hamish is a day trader who forms the opinion that JB Hi Fi will fall from its current price of
$18.50 to $18. He has $5000 to risk. The initial margin payment is 10%, ignoring brokerage costs.
(a) What CFD order should Hamish place and what exposure does this achieve?
(b) Later that day, JB Hi Fi shares are trading at $18. Explain how Hamish can close-out

(c)Hamish would close out by buying 2702 JB Hi Fi CFDs at $19.
This results in a loss of $49 987 – (2702 *19) = -$1351 so he would only receive back
$3649 of his deposit.

1. Explain the reasons for the growth of dark trading in Australia and the consequences of
such growth on market quality.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

ASIC (2013) found a number of reasons for the growth of dark trading. They include (1)

the main (lit) market as traders move from lit to dark market.

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Explain the effect of high-frequency trading (HFT) on market integrity and market

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

High frequency trading is computerised. It uses very fast computers with efficient trading
algorithms to analyse enormous amounts of data to detect arbitrage opportunities and then
automatically trades to take advantage of those opportunities. It results in a great number of
relatively small transactions.
There are concerns from regulators that the proliferation of high-frequency trading (HFT) has
negative effects on market integrity and market quality, especially after the occurrence of the
US ‘flash crash’ events (see Industry Insight 10.3). The ASIC has conducted a detailed review
of high-frequency trading in Australia and concluded that HFT did not cause a reduction in
liquidity or increases in volatility and their profit ($110 to $180 million over the 12 months to
March 2015) was a small price to pay for the increased liquidity that HFT supply to the
market.

</details>

### Trading and settlement arrangements

#### Short-Answer Questions

1. Describe the trading and settlement arrangements used in the Australian share market.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The market is operated by the Australian Securities Exchange Limited (ASX), which
supervises new listings and secondary market trading in listed securities.
Share trades on the ASX are executed and settled electronically based on orders entered either
by or through brokers who act as agents for their clients. These orders can be limit or at-
market orders. The trading system used now is ASX Trade which is made up of three
subsystems that aim to meet the needs of different user segments. The main trading platform
is called TradeMatch. All the systems apply the principle of priority trading, where orders are
ranked in price order and then by time at each price in the central limit order book. Trading is
conducted from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. each business day.
Once matched, the transaction is settled electronically by CHESS on a T + 3 basis. This
enables the companies share registries to be updated.

</details>
