# Duties to Clients Exercises

## Case 1: Identifying the Client

A bank manages a pension plan for a company facing a takeover. A manager buys overpriced company shares for the pension plan to support management and preserve the bank's commercial relationship.

Who is the actual client, and has the manager acted properly?

## Case 2: Fair Allocation

An offering is heavily oversubscribed. Morris allocates shares to himself first and distributes the remainder unevenly among clients. He also received options from the issuer but did not disclose them.

Identify the violations.

## Case 3: Suitability

Two clients have similar incomes, but one is older, has a smaller asset base, and a much lower risk tolerance. Their adviser recommends the same aggressive small-cap strategy to both.

Evaluate the recommendation.

## Case 4: Performance Presentation

A firm advertises steady annual growth of 25%, although only one year achieved 25% and the long-term average was 4%. It excludes several weak accounts from the presentation.

Which standards are implicated?

## Case 5: Confidentiality

A client gives an adviser confidential internal reports relating to a construction project. The adviser discloses the information to an outside party without permission, legal compulsion, or evidence of illegal conduct.

Has the adviser violated the standard?

## Answers and Analysis

1. The plan participants and beneficiaries are the actual clients. The manager violates Standard III(A) by subordinating their long-term interests to management and the bank's interests.
2. Morris violates Standard III(B) by failing to allocate fairly. The undisclosed issuer options also create a violation of Standard VI(A).
3. The recommendation is unsuitable for the lower-risk client. Standard III(C) requires assessment of each client's objectives, constraints, financial circumstances, and total portfolio.
4. The selective and inaccurate presentation violates Standard III(D). The implied assurance of steady future performance may also constitute misrepresentation under Standard I(C).
5. Yes. Confidentiality continues unless disclosure is authorized by the client, required by law, concerns client illegality, or is otherwise permitted under the applicable professional-conduct process.
