# Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions Exercises

## Case 1: Adequate Due Diligence

An investment bank expects many issuers to seek financing before a legal deadline. To save time, an analyst evaluates only a few representative firms and applies those findings to all prospective issuers.

Does the analyst have a reasonable basis for each recommendation?

## Case 2: Third-Party Research

A portfolio manager relies on an external research report without reviewing its assumptions, analytical rigor, publication date, or independence.

What duty has the manager overlooked?

## Case 3: Fact versus Opinion

After reviewing a small number of exploratory drilling samples, Richard Dox states as a fact that a property contains 500,000 ounces of gold.

How should the conclusion be presented?

## Case 4: Record Retention

An analyst deletes emails, model assumptions, and third-party research after publishing a recommendation. When leaving the firm, she keeps copies of selected client messages for future use.

Identify the problems.

## Answers and Analysis

1. No. The recommendation for each issuer lacks adequate investigation and violates Standard V(A). Time pressure does not justify omitting material firm-specific analysis.
2. Standard V(A) requires reasonable and diligent evaluation of third-party research, including assumptions, rigor, timeliness, and objectivity.
3. The estimate is a quantitative opinion based on samples and assumptions, not an established fact. Presenting it as fact violates Standard V(B).
4. Deleting supporting material violates Standard V(C). Records supporting professional work must be maintained under applicable law and firm policy, and firm records may not be taken for personal use when employment ends.
