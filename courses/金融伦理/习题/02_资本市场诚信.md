# Integrity of Capital Markets Exercises

## Case 1: Material Nonpublic Information

Staple learns confidentially that a listed company is about to be sold. He tells his broker, Halsey, who purchases the shares before the announcement.

1. Is the information material and nonpublic?
2. Who violates the standard?

## Case 2: Mosaic Theory

An analyst combines public filings, public satellite data, and a nonmaterial comment from a supplier to conclude that a company's sales will exceed consensus estimates. She trades for client accounts.

Is the trade automatically prohibited?

## Case 3: Restricted Social-Media Group

An analyst receives earnings information in an invitation-only online group. The information is not available through company filings, press releases, or other public channels.

How should the analyst treat the information?

## Case 4: Market Manipulation

John Gray divides a large position among multiple accounts, spreads false rumors online, and coordinates trades to create the appearance of strong demand and liquidity before selling.

Classify the conduct and identify the violation.

## Answers and Analysis

1. A pending sale is likely material because it can affect value, and it remains nonpublic until broadly disseminated. Staple violates Standard II(A) by communicating it, and Halsey violates the same standard by trading on it.
2. No. The mosaic theory permits a conclusion based on public information plus nonmaterial nonpublic information, provided no material nonpublic information is used.
3. Treat it as potentially nonpublic and material. The analyst should not trade or cause others to trade until the information has been broadly disseminated or is otherwise confirmed as public.
4. The false rumors are information-based manipulation, while the coordinated trades are transaction-based manipulation. Together they violate Standard II(B).
