# Conflicts of Interest Exercises

## Case 1: Paid Recommendation

An adviser receives additional compensation for recommending Badger stock but does not disclose the arrangement to clients.

Which standard is violated?

## Case 2: Personal Trading Priority

An analyst believes a security is undervalued. He purchases it for his personal account before submitting the recommendation for approval and before his employer or clients can trade.

Evaluate the sequence of transactions.

## Case 3: Beneficial Ownership

A family member's account is controlled by an analyst, who makes investment decisions and shares in the gains. The analyst treats the account as unrelated and places its orders alongside client orders without disclosure.

What issue arises?

## Case 4: Referral Fees

A brokerage firm refers clients to an investment counsellor and receives ongoing commissions. The counsellor accepts a prospective client without disclosing the arrangement, its duration, or estimated value.

Identify the violation and the appropriate disclosure.

## Answers and Analysis

1. Standard VI(A) is violated because the compensation could impair objectivity and must be fully and fairly disclosed.
2. The analyst violates Standard VI(B). Client and employer transactions must receive priority over transactions in which the professional is the beneficial owner.
3. The account involves beneficial ownership and must be treated as a personal account for disclosure, preclearance, and transaction-priority purposes.
4. Standard VI(C) is violated. Before a formal agreement, the client should receive clear disclosure of who pays or receives the benefit, whether it is cash or noncash, how it is calculated, whether it is one-time or continuing, and its estimated value.
