# Duties to Employers Exercises

## Case 1: Leaving an Employer

Before his resignation becomes effective, Samuel Magee contacts clients using his employer's records and asks them to move their accounts to his future firm.

Has Magee violated his duty of loyalty?

## Case 2: Independent Practice

An employee plans to provide paid investment services on weekends. She tells her employer only that she has a "personal project" and starts work before receiving consent.

What should she have done?

## Case 3: Additional Compensation

A client promises an analyst an overseas vacation if the portfolio earns at least 15%. The analyst accepts but does not notify the employer.

Identify the violation and the required procedure.

## Case 4: Supervisory Responsibility

A research director tells selected executives about an unpublished recommendation change. One executive immediately trades for himself and favored clients. The department head had no procedures to review such communications or trades.

Evaluate the department head's responsibility.

## Answers and Analysis

1. Yes. Soliciting clients before resignation takes effect and using employer records violate Standard IV(A). After leaving, contact may be permissible only when it does not rely on misappropriated records or breach enforceable agreements.
2. She should disclose the specific services, compensation, duration, and other terms and obtain the employer's consent before providing the services.
3. Standard IV(B) is violated. The analyst must make a written disclosure describing the nature, approximate value, duration, and conditions of the compensation and obtain written consent from all relevant parties before acceptance.
4. The department head violates Standard IV(C) if reasonable supervisory and compliance procedures were not established and enforced. Supervision includes prevention, monitoring, detection, investigation, and correction.
