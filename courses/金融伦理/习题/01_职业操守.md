# Professionalism Exercises

## Case 1: Knowledge of the Law

Michael Allen learns that financial statements filed by his firm with a regulator overstate an issuer's earnings. The firm's general counsel says that proving Allen's involvement would be difficult.

1. Which standard is most directly relevant?
2. What should Allen do?

## Case 2: Independence and Objectivity

Steven Taylor is invited by an issuer to inspect several remote mining facilities. The issuer arranges charter flights that are necessary to reach the sites and provides modest accommodation. Taylor also attends an ordinary meal with other analysts.

Does accepting these arrangements necessarily violate the standard? Explain.

## Case 3: Misrepresentation and Conflicts

Anthony McGuire publishes strong recommendations for companies that pay him to promote their shares. His website does not disclose these arrangements and presents him as an independent analyst.

Identify all relevant violations.

## Case 4: Misconduct

A trust officer repeatedly becomes intoxicated during the workday. Colleagues and clients question his ability to make investment decisions.

Does this conduct fall within the professional misconduct standard?

## Case 5: Competence

An analyst is assigned to assess a newly negotiated trade agreement. She reviews current reporting, consults specialists, and studies the agreement before issuing her research.

Has she met the competence requirement?

## Answers and Analysis

1. Standard I(A), Knowledge of the Law. Allen should report the matter to his supervisor, obtain independent legal or compliance advice, determine whether notification is legally required, and dissociate from the activity if it is not remedied.
2. Not necessarily. Necessary group transportation and modest hospitality may be accepted when they do not compromise, or appear reasonably likely to compromise, independent judgment. Taylor should still consider firm policy and disclosure requirements.
3. McGuire violates Standard I(C) because omitting the paid relationship creates a misleading impression. He also violates Standard VI(A) by failing to disclose the conflict of interest.
4. Yes. The behavior raises serious questions about professional integrity and competence and therefore violates Standard I(D).
5. Yes. Her efforts to update her knowledge, consult experts, and understand the agreement demonstrate reasonable professional competence under Standard I(E).
