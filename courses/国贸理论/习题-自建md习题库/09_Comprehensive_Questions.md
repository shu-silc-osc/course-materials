17. A country is labour-abundant and cloth is labour-intensive. Predict the trade pattern and identify a group that could lose from the resulting price change.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The country tends to export cloth. If trade lowers the domestic relative price of food or raises the relative price of cloth, factor returns change according to the relevant factor-intensity mechanism; owners of a factor used intensively in import-competing production may lose. State the price change before naming winners and losers.

18. A government proposes subsidising a new export industry because it may learn and lower costs over time. Give the economic rationale and two policy risks.

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Dynamic economies and learning by doing may create future cost reductions that private firms cannot fully capture. Risks include government inability to identify a viable industry, lobbying or capture, fiscal cost, and retaliation. The expected external benefit must exceed these costs.

</details>

19. A government claims an import surge proves dumping and asks for an anti-dumping duty. What additional evidence is needed?

> Difficulty: Basic

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Evidence of dumping under the applicable comparison, material injury or threat to the domestic industry, and a causal link, established through the required investigation and procedure.

</details>

20. A firm moves customer service operations abroad but continues to sell the same product domestically. Explain why this is service outsourcing and identify one reason the activity may remain local.

> Difficulty: Intermediate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The firm has relocated a service operation across borders, which is service outsourcing/offshoring. The service may need face-to-face contact or proximity to customers, making remote provision impractical.
