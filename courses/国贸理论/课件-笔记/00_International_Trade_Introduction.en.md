# Introduction to International Trade | Notes

> [!abstract] Learning objectives
> Establish the scope of international economics, distinguish international trade from international finance, and understand the course structure and assessment.

## 1. Scope of the subject

International economics studies interactions among nations through trade in goods and services, money flows, and investment. As national economies become more connected, these interactions become increasingly important.

| Branch | Main transactions | Core questions |
|---|---|---|
| International trade | Cross-border goods and services | Why trade occurs, trade patterns, gains from trade, and trade policy |
| International finance | Cross-border monetary and financial transactions | Balance of payments, exchange-rate determination, policy coordination, and capital markets |

Trade theory explains the basis, pattern, and benefits of trade. Trade policy studies how governments affect trade. The introductory framework considers trade from international, national, firm, and individual perspectives.

## 2. Learning and assessment

- Teaching combines lectures, group discussion, and practical workshops. Lectures provide the structure, explain theory, and introduce examples.
- Assessment: class performance and exercises 10%; individual presentation 15%; group report 15%; closed-book examination 60%.
- The examination is a two-hour closed-book exam during the examination week.
- Groups generally have five or six members, select one country, and use it as the shared case for discussion, presentations, and the final report.

## 3. Framework diagram

The course map separates international economics into trade and finance. Trade is divided into theory and policy. Finance covers monetary theory, financial policy, and related institutions. For any problem, first identify whether the main object is a real transaction or a financial transaction, then assess its effects at the international, national, firm, or individual level.

## Check yourself

- Goods and services crossing borders belong to trade analysis; exchange rates, balance-of-payments issues, and capital flows belong to finance.
- Aggregate gains from trade are distinct from how those gains are distributed among domestic groups.
- Can you distinguish the basis, pattern, and benefits of trade?
