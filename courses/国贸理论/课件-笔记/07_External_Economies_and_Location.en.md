# Chapter 7: External Economies of Scale and the International Location of Production

> [!abstract] Learning objectives
> Distinguish internal from external economies of scale and explain how industrial clusters and historical path dependence affect production location and trade.

## Scale economies and industry structure

Constant returns to scale mean that output rises in the same proportion as inputs. Increasing returns mean that output rises more than proportionally. Internal economies lower a firm’s average cost as that firm expands and are often associated with imperfect competition. External economies lower costs for firms when the industry grows, even if individual firms do not expand.

External economies can arise from specialised suppliers, pooled labour markets, and knowledge spillovers. A larger local cluster improves supply chains, access to specialised workers, and exchange of experience. Figures 7-1 and 7-2 relate industry scale to average cost and supply under external economies.

## Trade, history, and lock-in

External economies can produce a downward-sloping industry supply curve. An early lead, perhaps caused by historical accident, can attract firms and suppliers. Once a cluster forms, production may remain there even if another location has better underlying resources. Trade expands the market and supports clustering, but may also lock production into an established location. Figures 7-3–7-5 illustrate prices, industry scale, and location advantages before and after trade.

Dynamic economies arise through learning by doing: cumulative output can lower unit cost. Figure 7-6 plots a learning curve, with cost declining as cumulative production increases. First-mover advantages can motivate infant-industry policies, but governments may not identify future winners and policy costs may exceed any gains.

## Check yourself

- Internal economies concern a firm’s own scale; external economies concern cost effects from industry clustering.
- A low-cost location may result from history and need not be the location with the best current natural endowment.
- Can you explain falling costs through suppliers, labour pools, knowledge spillovers, and learning?

competitive.

become imperfectly competitive.

• What will happen when the countries open up the

### Slide 19

External Economies and International

• Chinese button prices were lower than U.S. button

• Because China’s supply curve is forward-falling, 
increased production as a result of trade leads to a 
button price that is lower than the price before

• Trade leads to prices that are lower than the prices 
in either country before trade!

### Slide 20

External Economies and International 
Trade (cont.)
• Very different from the implications of models

• In the standard trade model relative prices 
converge as a result of trade. 
• If cloth is relatively cheap in the home country and 
relatively expensive in the foreign country before

• With external economies, by contrast, the effect of

Dynamic Increasing Returns
• So far, we have considered cases where external 
economies depend on the amount of current output 
at a point in time.
• But external economies may also depend on the 
amount of cumulative output over time.
• Dynamic increasing returns to scale exist if 
average costs fall as cumulative output over time 
rises.
– Dynamic increasing returns to scale imply dynamic 
external economies of scale.

### Slide 34

1-34 
Summary
1. Trade need not be the result of comparative 
advantage. Instead, it can result from increasing 
returns or economies of scale, that is, from a 
tendency of unit costs to be lower with larger 
output. 
2. Economies of scale give countries an incentive to 
specialize and trade even in the absence of 
differences in resources or technology between 
countries.

### Slide 35

1-35 
Summary (cont.)
3. Economies of scale can be internal (depending on 
the size of the firm) or external (depending on the 
size of the industry).
4. Economies of scale can lead to a breakdown of 
perfect competition, unless they take the form of 
external economies, which occur at the level of 
the industry instead of the firm.

### Slide 36

1-36 
Summary (cont.)
5. External economies give an important role to 
history and accident in determining the pattern of 
international trade. 
• When external economies are important, a country 
starting with a large advantage may retain that 
advantage even if another country could potentially 
produce the same goods more cheaply.

### Slide 37

1-37 
Summary (cont.)
6. When external economies are important, 
countries can conceivably lose from trade. 
• Also the free trade price can fall below the price before 
trade in both countries.
7. Trade based on external economies of scale may 
increase or decrease national welfare, and 
countries may benefit from temporary 
protectionism if their industries exhibit external 
economies of scale either at a point in time or 
over time.
